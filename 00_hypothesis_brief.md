# Formate → Insulin Synthesis: Research Brief

Source: *The Spice of Life*, Alexei Vazquez (2020). Core statement in Ch. 7
"Formate theory of diabetes" (book pp. 30–31). Amazon: B084QLM79X.

## The hypothesis (as stated in the book)

**Formate stimulates the synthesis of insulin in the pancreas**, and thereby
strengthens the insulin response to high blood glucose.

The book is explicit that this is theory, not yet demonstrated:
> "There is no experimental proof to this line of reasoning. For now, it is
> just a theory."

## The mechanistic chain (the argument to be tested)

1. Insulin is a protein hormone — a 51-amino-acid chain. Pancreatic β-cells
   *synthesize* it; secretion of a fresh batch is therefore an **extreme
   anabolic event** (framed via David Ron's 2019 Beatson keynote: "insulin
   secretion is an extreme anabolic process").
2. Protein synthesis is controlled by **mTOR**, the master regulator of
   anabolism.
3. **mTOR is activated by adenine nucleotides / purines** (Hoxhaj et al., 2017,
   Cell Rep — mTORC1 senses cellular purine nucleotide levels).
4. **Formate stimulates adenosine (purine) synthesis** — established earlier in
   the book (Ch. 4 "The metabolic switch"; Oizel et al., 2019). Formate donates
   2 of the carbons of the purine ring; it also stimulates mTOR directly and
   inhibits AMPK (favors anabolism, opposes catabolism).
5. **By extrapolation:** formate → ↑purine synthesis → ↑mTOR → ↑insulin
   synthesis → stronger, faster insulin response to glucose.

Reverse direction (formate theory of diabetes):
- Formate deficiency → anabolic deficiency → ↓mTOR → **compromised insulin
  response** → elevated blood glucose → obesity/diabetes risk.

## Supporting observations offered in the book

- **Self-experiment (glucose tolerance test, Ch. 6, pp. 27–29).** 50 g glucose
  ± 1 g sodium formate, author as sole subject, morning, 8 vs 12 replicates,
  one-tail t-test.
  | Time (min) | Glucose alone (g/L) | Glucose + 1 g formate (g/L) |
  |-----------:|--------------------:|----------------------------:|
  | 0   | 1.10 | 1.09 |
  | 30  | 1.63 | 1.42 |
  | 60  | 1.59 | 1.55 |
  | 90  | 1.10 | 1.21 |
  | 120 | 0.96 | 0.98 |
  Formate blunted the 30-min peak (1.63 → 1.42, p≈0.03); hypoglycemic index
  0.97 (p≈0.0003). Interpreted as formate accelerating glucose clearance,
  consistent with (but not proof of) an enhanced insulin response.
- **Spanish cohort (Pietzke et al., 2019).** Blood formate low in cancer,
  diabetes, and obese subjects vs healthy; glucose–formate seesaw. Association
  only.
- Epidemiology consistent with "more formate → less diabetes": coffee/caffeine
  (→ formate) lowers diabetes risk; antibiotics and statins (→ ↓formate) raise
  diabetes risk.

## Key tension / counter-evidence to keep in view

- **Carpene et al., 2019** (in the book's own bibliography): "Methylamine
  Activates Glucose Uptake in Human Adipocytes **Without Overpassing Action of
  Insulin or Stimulating its Secretion in Pancreatic Islets**." Title implies a
  formate-precursor did NOT stimulate insulin secretion in islets — must be
  reconciled. Note this is about *secretion*, whereas the book's claim is about
  *synthesis*; the distinction (synthesis vs secretion vs peripheral glucose
  uptake) matters and the self-experiment cannot separate them.
- The glucose-lowering in the self-experiment is equally compatible with a
  **peripheral / insulin-independent** effect (e.g., formate/methylamine
  enhancing glucose uptake in muscle/fat directly) rather than pancreatic
  insulin synthesis. Carpene's adipocyte result actually supports this
  alternative.

## What is NOT yet established (open questions for the investigation)

1. Does formate increase insulin *synthesis* (proinsulin/insulin content,
   INS transcription/translation) in β-cells? — no direct data.
2. Does formate increase insulin *secretion* (GSIS) from islets/β-cell lines? —
   Carpene suggests methylamine does not; formate untested directly.
3. Is any in-vivo glucose effect insulin-dependent vs peripheral? — unresolved.
4. Is the mTOR→insulin-synthesis link in β-cells real and formate-sensitive?
5. Human causal evidence beyond a single-subject n=1 self-experiment.
